# AnyDock Project Context

## Purpose and product behavior

AnyDock receives images, UTF-8 text snippets, HTTP/HTTPS links and arbitrary files shared from an iPhone over the local network and exposes them in a native macOS menu bar tray. Users can browse thumbnails, select images, drag their original files into another app, copy them, preview them with Quick Look or reveal them in Finder.

The tray is a 480 × 640 pt popover with a fixed four-column masonry layout; the Text filter uses a single full-width column. The top row holds a single glass filter bar with a sliding selection indicator at the left and one glass capsule group with the Folder and Settings buttons at the right, both aligned with the cards' 24 pt margins; the filter bar scrolls horizontally when its segments do not fit beside the group. The first row rests below the top row, and content scrolls directly under it and the bottom floating controls. Cards are 12 pt apart. The filters show All, Images, Text, Links and Files with counts, hide empty types, default to All and switch back to All when a new arrival would be hidden. Cards go into the shortest column, with ties going left. Heights preserve aspect ratio up to 2.5 times the column width; capped images show their top portion with aspect fill. Liquid Glass is used on macOS 26 or later, with a native visual-effect fallback on older supported systems.

The tray shows all four content kinds: frameless image cards, and Polaroid-style cards for text (first lines), links (one-line domain and URL) and files (Quick Look first page or monospaced first lines). Polaroid cards have one caption line that is never clipped: the middle-truncated filename for files and the receipt time for text and links. Every card carries a colored extension badge at its top-left. Dragging gives the original file for images and files, plain text for text, and the URL plus the file for links. Receipt time appears on image, text and link cards; the latest receipt is marked by an accent dot, independent of the selection border. A centered floating selection and drop capsule sits above the clipboard bar (or near the bottom edge when the bar is hidden) and never changes the content area. With one item selected it shows that item's detail: `name · size · time` for files (only the name is middle-truncated), `PNG · 1.2 MB · 21:30` for images (never the stored filename), `86 chars · 21:51` / `86 字 · 21:51` for text and the middle-truncated full URL for links; with several items it shows the count. Configuration and listener status belong in Settings. New receipts reveal or update the tray and scroll into view; loading history does not reveal it. Each manual opening rereads the receive folder, reflecting Finder removals and restores, while receipt-driven opening skips an extra reload. Every actual tray close clears the selection. Quick Look keeps the tray open; closing only Quick Look preserves selection.

There is no trash button and no way to recycle a whole filter at once. With a selection, the floating capsule shows `N selected` / `已选 N 项`; with none it is hidden. While cards are dragged from the tray, the capsule turns into a red `Drop here to move to Trash` / `拖到这里放入废纸篓` target that highlights when the pointer enters; dropping there recycles exactly the dragged items without confirmation, clears the selection and keeps the tray open. The red target hides as soon as the mouse button is released, without waiting for the drop or slide-back animation. Drops elsewhere behave as before. Single-clicking a filter segment switches to it; double-clicking also selects every card in that filter (All selects every card), or clears the selection when every visible card is already selected; it never inverts a partial selection. Command-Delete and the context menu recycle selected items directly. Every request is checked against the current receive folder's `ImageStore.list` set and uses `NSWorkspace.recycle`. Originals are never overwritten or deleted automatically.

A separate glass clipboard bar sits at the bottom of the tray, 16 pt above the edge, with its outer edges aligned to the cards' 24 pt margins; it is not part of the filters or counts. While the General switch `Keep last 4 clipboard texts` (UserDefaults `keepClipboardHistory`, on by default) is on, AnyDock polls `NSPasteboard.general.changeCount` about every 0.5 s and records plain text, including text AnyDock copied itself, skipping file URLs and `org.nspasteboard.ConcealedType` content but not transient content. Copying the connection key in Settings is marked concealed. It keeps the 4 newest entries in memory only, skips a repeat of the newest one and is cleared when the app quits or the switch is turned off. Before AnyDock writes to the clipboard it checks once synchronously, so text just written by another app is captured first. The bar is hidden while the history is empty and is collapsed at every tray opening: one capsule row with `Clipboard · N items` / `剪贴板 · N 条`, Clear and an up arrow; clicking the row or arrow expands it to `Clipboard · Double-click to copy` / `剪贴板 · 双击复制` with four equal cells (two lines of text and a relative time, newest at the left with an accent dot); clicking its header row (except Clear) or the down arrow collapses it again. Double-clicking a cell copies it again without adding a duplicate; cells drag out as plain text; Escape collapses first; Clear empties the history immediately without confirmation.

Settings has General, Connection and Receiving toolbar tabs, with General selected initially. General owns the off-by-default option to copy each new item to the clipboard, an optional global wake shortcut that toggles the tray from any app, appearance, language, immediate login startup and Quit. Appearance follows the system or forces Light/Dark using `NSApp.appearance`, applying immediately to the tray and Settings; the tray popover copies `NSApp.effectiveAppearance` because its menu bar anchor does not inherit the app appearance. Language follows the system or selects English/Simplified Chinese via the app-local `AppleLanguages` override and requires a restart; Settings offers Restart Now. Connection owns one-step AirDrop setup and manual address/key copying plus generic shortcut export. The one-step shortcut contains the applied address and key; its editable name persists in UserDefaults and defaults to localized `Send to ` / `发送到 ` plus the computer name, falling back to localized `Send to Mac`. Saved custom names are preserved. Each Mac sends its own named shortcut. Only Receiving stages folder/port edits and has an Apply button; receiver status and failures appear there. Switching folders never moves or deletes previous originals.

See `docs/PROJECT.md` for the complete current UI and engineering rules.

## Technical foundation

- Swift 6 language mode, SwiftPM tools version 6.0, macOS 14+ deployment target. The development toolchain is Xcode 27.2 / Apple Swift 6.4.2; source builds need a compatible compiler and an SDK containing the macOS 26 glass APIs.
- Native AppKit controls, materials, selection, dragging, Quick Look and sharing APIs. No third-party package dependencies.
- Network.framework HTTP receiver, default TCP port 8765, configurable in Settings. No automatic port switching.
- Received originals live in the user-selected directory. Fresh configurations default to `~/Downloads/AnyDock`; existing configured paths are unchanged. New items use Images/Text/Links/Files subdirectories, while flat legacy images remain in place. Receiver configuration lives at `Application Support/AnyDock/configuration.json`; appearance, language, automatic clipboard copy, the wake shortcut (`wakeHotKey`) and personalized shortcut names use UserDefaults, retaining legacy preference keys.
- `Package.swift` is the package source of truth. Project scripts assemble an ad-hoc-signed app bundle with identifier `com.cuxiao.mac.anydock`.

## Structure

- `Sources/AnyDockCore`: compatible authenticated multi-type upload protocol, typed storage/listing, quarantine protection, pure masonry geometry and isolated configuration migration.
- `Sources/AnyDock`: app lifecycle, tray layout and interactions, Settings and personalized shortcut preparation.
- `Resources/Info.plist`: application metadata.
- `Sources/AnyDock/Resources`: English and Simplified Chinese `.lproj` strings, plural rules and localized privacy text. English is the development language; SwiftPM resources use `Bundle.module` and `build.sh` embeds the resource bundle in the app. Shortcut-internal prompts generated by Python remain Chinese.
- `Resources/Shortcuts`: signed generic manual shortcut; `build.sh` also generates the unsigned personalization template here.
- `scripts`: build, run, lint, test and shortcut generation entry points. Approved rendered app and menu bar icon assets are packaged directly; see `Resources/Brand/README.md` for provenance.
- `Tests`: protocol, storage, masonry placement and shortcut checks.
- `docs/PROJECT.md`: engineering operations, UI rules and debugging commands.

## Commands

Run from the project root. No package installation is required; development scripts use system Python 3.

- `./scripts/lint.sh`: check formatting, script syntax and bundle metadata.
- `./scripts/test.sh`: run Swift and shortcut checks.
- `./scripts/build.sh`: create and ad-hoc sign `dist/AnyDock.app`. It regenerates the unsigned `Resources/Shortcuts/Template.shortcut`, which is ignored by Git.
- `./scripts/run.sh`: build and open the menu bar app when launching is requested.

GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs lint, test and build on the `macos-26` runner with its newest installed Xcode. `scripts/shortcut.sh` signs the generic shortcut with `shortcuts sign`, which needs a signed-in Mac; run it locally and commit the signed `Resources/Shortcuts/发送到 Mac.shortcut`. CI never signs.

Pull requests merge through `.github/workflows/auto-merge.yml`; authors never merge by hand. A PR is squash-merged when its current head is the approved commit, no reviewer's latest decision is `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, counting only reviewers with write, maintain or admin permission on the repository, CI has run and every other check succeeded, and it has no `needs-acceptance` label. A PR with visible UI changes gets `needs-acceptance` when it is opened; the architect removes it after the user accepts the UI, which retries the merge. After merging, the workflow dispatches CI on `main`, because merges made with `GITHUB_TOKEN` do not trigger push CI. See `docs/PROJECT.md` for details.

## Trust and contribution boundaries

The receiver is intended for a trusted LAN. HTTP does not encrypt images or the installation-specific connection key; do not expose the listener to the Internet. Validate request sizes and image contents and generate unique filenames. Limit user-requested recycling to generated items in the current folder; the tray and recycling authorization use the same typed list. Every non-image is quarantined, and received content is never automatically opened or executed. Tests use temporary directories, fixture credentials and separate ports; never print or publish real keys, configuration or received images.

Personalized shortcuts contain the connection key. Prepare and sign them only in temporary storage, clean up after sharing or failure, and never put them into the source tree or bundle. System `shortcuts sign --mode anyone` submits a copy to Apple for validation; Settings discloses this and directs users to their own iPhone. Signing or AirDrop failures leave manual setup available. Changing the receive port requires resending or updating the iPhone shortcut.

Updated shortcuts send Share Sheet items one at a time or use the clipboard when there is no input. Oversize and HTTP failures are counted and reported once after the loop. Transport failures may abort through the normal iOS error behavior; there is no dependency or persistent retry queue. Resend the shortcut after upgrading.

Follow `AGENTS.md` for contribution conventions and `docs/PROJECT.md` for current behavior. Respect the accepted task scope and report the checks actually performed. Local checks do not replace visual, iPhone or target-chat acceptance.

## Rename compatibility

Use `com.cuxiao.mac.anydock` for the bundle identifier, Logger subsystem and current preferences domain. Preserve existing preference keys, including copyNewItems and personalizedShortcutName. Keep configured receive paths unchanged; fresh installations use ~/Downloads/AnyDock. Before UI initialization, copy the legacy preferences and `Application Support/SnapDock/configuration.json` into the new domain and `Application Support/AnyDock/configuration.json`, leaving old data intact. Do not overwrite an existing new configuration file or existing preference values; only mark migration complete after success. On failure, retain old data, do not mark completion, and retry at the next launch. The app displays in-memory defaults with an empty key, pauses receiving, prevents saving or personalizing that fallback, and shows a safe localized migration warning in Receiving. Never log configuration bytes or keys. Login items are not migrated automatically; users must switch launch at login off and on after upgrading. New files use `AnyDock-`; `ImageStore.list` must also include legacy `SnapDock-` images, and recycling must use that same set. Retain the `X-SnapDock-Key` upload header so installed phone shortcuts keep working. Product names are localized as AnyDock in English and 随手坞 in Simplified Chinese.
